# Stakeholder negotiation

Clarify what the user can decide, what requires another owner's agreement, and what is already committed. Separate stakeholder positions from the interests and constraints supported by evidence.

1. Establish the outcome, negotiable variables, hard constraints, decision authority, and a credible alternative if agreement is not reached. Do not invent a competing offer or threat.
2. Build feasible packages rather than isolated concessions: scope, date, staffing, quality, cost, and risk move together. State the assumptions that make each package viable.
3. Check consistency. If scope expands while date and capacity stay fixed, identify the resulting tradeoff explicitly. Do not convert pressure into an unsupported promise.
4. Prepare a concise opening, evidence for the constraints, questions that reveal interests, and conditional concessions. Explain material risks plainly; do not conceal them to obtain agreement.
5. Close with exact scope, owner, delivery or review date, acceptance evidence, dependencies, and unresolved conditions. Distinguish a proposed package, verbal indication, and confirmed agreement.

Deliver an options comparison and a usable discussion or written proposal. For an existing agreement, test whether each party would describe the obligations the same way. Drafting does not authorize sending, signing, or changing project commitments.
