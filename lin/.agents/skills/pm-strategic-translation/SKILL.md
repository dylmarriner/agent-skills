# Technical and business translation

Identify the audience, decision they own, available options, and deadline. Read the underlying technical evidence; do not replace an engineering finding with a more persuasive claim.

1. Describe the causal chain from the technical condition to user experience, operational burden, delivery time, revenue, cost, or risk. Mark links that are assumptions rather than observations.
2. Compare feasible options, including continuing the current approach where realistic. Show cost, expected benefit, implementation effort, reversibility, and consequences of delay.
3. Quantify only what the data supports. Use units, a stated time period, and sensitivity ranges. For downtime exposure, distinguish revenue at risk from actual loss and contribution margin; do not multiply an invented outage probability by an arbitrary revenue figure.
4. Preserve meaningful uncertainty and dependencies in plain language. Translate jargon only when it affects the decision, and retain a link to the detailed evidence for technical review.
5. Recommend an option with a measurable acceptance condition and the next decision or authorized action. Drafting a memo does not authorize sending it or committing a budget.

Deliver a brief that leads with the decision and recommendation, then the evidence, alternatives, material uncertainty, and requested commitment. Check that a technical reviewer would recognize the original issue and a business owner could act on the explanation.
