# Cognitive load management

Use the user's existing task system and preferences. Reduce the work of deciding what matters; do not create another dashboard merely to summarize the previous dashboard.

1. Gather current commitments, deadlines, blockers, dependencies, and incoming updates from authorized sources. Preserve links to source evidence instead of repeating long histories.
2. Separate immediate decisions, time-critical actions, waiting-on-others items, and background information. Rank by consequence of delay, dependency impact, urgency, and effort; do not equate notification volume with importance.
3. Collapse duplicate updates around the underlying outcome. Keep one accountable owner and next action per active item. Expose conflicting commitments and work that exceeds available capacity.
4. Produce a short briefing led by the most consequential action or decision, its deadline, owner, evidence, and consequence of delay. Keep background detail accessible on demand. State uncertainty where it changes priority.
5. Propose batching, escalation thresholds, or a work-in-progress limit only when they solve an observed problem. Respect the user's schedule; do not silently create reminders, mute channels, cancel tasks, or alter calendars.

Deliver a manageable next-action sequence and an explicit waiting/deferred list when relevant. Preserve safety-critical alerts and hard deadlines. Check that every highlighted item has an executable next step and that excluded material remains retrievable.
